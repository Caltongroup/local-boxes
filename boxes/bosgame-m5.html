What it is

Bosgame’s sealed Ryzen AI Max+ 395 mini PC. 16 Zen 5 cores, Radeon 8060S (40 RDNA 3.5 CUs), soldered LPDDR5X-8000 in 96GB or 128GB, dual M.2 PCIe 4.0, Windows 11 Pro. Same Strix Halo silicon as the Framework Desktop and the GMKtec EVO-X2. This page is the 128GB machine.

Who it is for

People who want a 24B–70B local model and a real Windows PC already assembled, will pick 128GB, and can live with llama.cpp / ROCm / Vulkan plus a loud performance-mode fan. Wrong if your tools assume CUDA, if you want to repair the box, if you need a quiet desk, or if you buy 96GB and expect large local models.

What it actually runs

  • Comfortable: 24B–32B dense at usable speed on the iGPU. Same class as EVO-X2.
  • Possible: 70B-class dense and large MoE on 128GB if you accept that ~256 GB/s is not Mac Studio bandwidth. Capacity is the win. Decode is not.
  • Not the win: training, CUDA-only stacks, or beating a 5090 on anything that already fits in 32GB VRAM. Ignore vendor token-rate slides.

Standout details

  • Price snapshot 7 Sep 2026, official US store bosgamepc.com: 128GB + 2TB listed at $2,999 (struck-through $3,699). 96GB + 2TB is also sold. Treat $2,999 as a snapshot, not a promise. Other sites show different or lower figures, sometimes duty-excluded — this page uses the official US store.
  • Memory is soldered. Pick 128GB now or do not buy this for large models. BIOS can allocate up to 96GB of 128GB to iGPU cache (vendor FAQ).
  • 256-bit LPDDR5X-8000, ~256 GB/s theoretical. Same limiter as Framework and EVO-X2. Not a Mac Studio / discrete CUDA decode play.
  • Ports verified from the US store and reviews: dual USB4, HDMI + DisplayPort, one 2.5GbE LAN, Wi-Fi 7. Some marketing copy says dual LAN; the store connectivity copy lists a single 2.5Gbps LAN port. No 10GbE.
  • Three power modes. Sustained ~120W in performance mode, short burst higher. Reviews (Wccftech, Notebookcheck, Starry Hope) put performance-mode noise in the ~50 dB class. That fan is the catch.
  • Vendor FAQ discusses OCuLink port alignment. Reviews and spec sheets do not treat this as a shipping eGPU box. Do not buy it for that.
  • One-year warranty. Brand and support are thinner than Framework or HP. Sealed chassis.

Honest verdict

Buy the 128GB M5 if you want sealed Strix Halo already built and the official US-store ticket is near $2,999. Skip 96GB for this job. Performance mode is loud. If you want to repair the machine, get Framework Desktop. If you want another sealed Halo cube, see EVO-X2. If you want CUDA, shop Spark / GX10.
