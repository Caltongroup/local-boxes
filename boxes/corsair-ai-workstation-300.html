What it is

Corsair’s compact Strix Halo workstation. Top config is the 16-core Ryzen AI Max+ 395 with Radeon 8060S (40 RDNA 3.5 CUs), up to 128GB of soldered LPDDR5X-8000, dual M.2 NVMe (up to 4TB), 2.5GbE, Wi-Fi 6E, dual USB4, HDMI + DisplayPort, SD card reader, and an internal ~300W Flex ATX PSU in a 4.4 L aluminum chassis. Ships with Windows 11 Home and a two-year warranty. Memory is soldered; you pick 64GB (Max 385) or 128GB (Max+ 395) at order time. This page is the 128GB machine.

Who it is for

People who want a 24B–70B local model already assembled under a known brand name, with dual storage slots and a real Windows PC, and who can live with ROCm / llama.cpp / Vulkan. Useful if you prefer Corsair support over no-name minis. Wrong if your tools assume CUDA, if you need the lowest street price for this silicon, or if you expect discrete-GPU token rates.

What it actually runs

  • Comfortable: 24B–32B dense at usable speed on the iGPU. Same class as Framework, EVO-X2, and the other Max+ 395 boxes.
  • Possible: 70B-class dense and larger MoE (120B-class reported) on 128GB if you accept that ~256 GB/s is the ceiling. Capacity is the win; decode is not.
  • Not the win: training, CUDA-only stacks, or beating a GB10 / 5090 on workloads that already fit in discrete VRAM. Tom’s Hardware found it trailing Nvidia’s GB10 in prompt processing and longer-context generation. Ignore vendor token-rate slides.

Standout details

  • Price snapshot 8 Sep 2026: official Corsair US store lists the 128GB / 1TB Max+ 395 around $4,599 (down from a higher list); 64GB Max 385 around $1,699. Launch prices in 2025 were ~$1,999–$2,299 for 128GB configs. Memory and NAND costs pushed the ticket up. Treat current figures as a snapshot. B&H and other channels sometimes show the base SKU lower.
  • Memory is soldered LPDDR5X-8000. Up to 96GB can be allocated to the iGPU. Pick 128GB or do not buy this for large models.
  • Same ~256 GB/s bandwidth ceiling as every other Strix Halo mini. Not a Mac Studio or discrete CUDA decode play.
  • Dual M.2 slots, dual USB4, 2.5GbE, SD 4.0 reader, internal PSU. Expansion is limited to storage. Chassis opens for SSD swaps.
  • Two-year warranty and Corsair support network. Preloaded AI software suite (LM Studio-friendly tools). Reviews note ROCm stack immaturity and occasional stability issues under creative workloads.
  • Fans are competent for the form factor but not silent under sustained load. Same thermal reality as the other sealed cubes.

Honest verdict

The brand-name prebuilt Strix Halo mini. Buy the 128GB config only if the current street ticket still makes sense against Framework, Bosgame, EVO-X2, or HP Z2 Mini. Same bandwidth ceiling as all of them. Price hikes after launch hurt the value case. If you want CUDA capacity, shop Spark / GX10. If you want repairability, get Framework Desktop.
