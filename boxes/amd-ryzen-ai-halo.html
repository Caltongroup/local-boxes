What it is

AMD’s first-party AI developer platform. Ryzen AI Max+ 395 (16 Zen 5 cores / 32 threads, Radeon 8060S with 40 RDNA 3.5 CUs, 50 TOPS XDNA 2 NPU), 128GB LPDDR5X-8000 unified memory, 2TB PCIe Gen4 SSD, 10GbE, Wi-Fi 7, HDMI 2.1b, USB-C ports, USB-C PD power, in a ~150 × 150 × 45 mm chassis under 1.2 kg. Ships with either Windows 11 Pro or a Linux image, preloaded AMD Developer Center app, ROCm stack, and playbooks for LM Studio, ComfyUI, llama.cpp, vLLM, and PyTorch. Memory is soldered. TDP up to ~120 W.

Who it is for

Developers who want a turnkey Strix Halo box with AMD’s Best Known Configuration, dual-OS support, and official playbooks rather than another no-name mini. Useful if you prefer buying the reference platform and living in ROCm / llama.cpp / Vulkan on Windows or Linux. Wrong if you need CUDA, if you want the lowest street price for this silicon, or if you are nowhere near a Micro Center (in-store pickup only, no shipping).

What it actually runs

  • Comfortable: 24B–32B dense at usable speed on the iGPU. Same class as Framework Desktop, Corsair AI Workstation 300, EVO-X2, Bosgame M5, and the other Max+ 395 boxes.
  • Possible: 70B-class dense and larger MoE (up to ~200B parameter class claimed with quantization) on 128GB if you accept that ~256 GB/s is the ceiling. Capacity is the win; decode is not discrete-GPU fast.
  • Not the win: training, CUDA-only stacks, or beating a GB10 / 5090 on workloads that already fit in discrete VRAM. Bandwidth matches every other Strix Halo mini.

Standout details

  • Price snapshot 9 Sep 2026: Micro Center lists both Windows 11 Pro and Linux SKUs at $3,999.99. Exclusive to Micro Center, in-store pickup only. Treat as a fixed list price, not a street discount.
  • Same ~256 GB/s LPDDR5X-8000 ceiling as every other Strix Halo 128GB box. Not a Mac Studio or discrete CUDA decode play.
  • 10GbE, Wi-Fi 7, dual-OS choice, official Developer Center with Best Known Configuration, and AMD playbooks. That is the software differentiator versus cheaper partner minis.
  • Form factor is paperback-sized and USB-C powered. Expansion is essentially zero beyond the fixed 2TB SSD.
  • Cheaper Strix Halo 128GB options (Bosgame, EVO-X2, Framework, etc.) exist under this ticket. The Halo premium buys the first-party stack and dual-OS support, not faster silicon.

Honest verdict

AMD’s reference Strix Halo developer box. Buy it if you want the official ROCm / playbook experience and live near a Micro Center. Same bandwidth and model class as the sealed minis and Framework Desktop; the $4k list is higher than several of those. If you want CUDA capacity, shop Spark / GX10. If you want the same chip cheaper or repairable, look at Bosgame, EVO-X2, or Framework Desktop.
